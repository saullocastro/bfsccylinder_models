\begin{abstract}
This note documents the implementation of the single-mode Koiter method with a
nonlinear pre-buckling state in
\texttt{koiter\_cylinder\_newton\_raphson.py} (von K\'arm\'an kinematics) and
\texttt{koiter\_cylinder\_newton\_raphson\_sanders.py} (Sanders kinematics).
It covers the incremental--iterative algorithm that locates the expansion
point in the neighbourhood of the bifurcation load, the consistency
requirements on the operators used in the eigenvalue problem and in the
asymptotic expansion, and the stiffness-weighted orthogonality condition
imposed on the second-order fields.  Equation numbers of the form
``Sun~Eq.~(nn)'' refer to Sun, Tian, Li and Wang, \emph{Accelerated Koiter
method for post-buckling analysis of thin-walled shells under axial
compression}, Thin-Walled Structures \textbf{155} (2020) 106962.
\end{abstract}

%==============================================================================
\section{Scope}
%==============================================================================

The routine \texttt{fkoiter\_cyl\_SS3} performs, for a circular cylindrical
shell under axial compression with SS-3 boundary conditions,
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item a pre-buckling analysis,
  \item a buckling eigenvalue analysis about the pre-buckling state,
  \item the Koiter asymptotic expansion about the bifurcation point, returning
        the post-buckling coefficients $a_{ijk}$ and $b_{ijkl}$.
\end{enumerate}

With \texttt{NLprebuck=False} the pre-buckling state is the linear elastic
solution and the pre-buckling path is exactly linear in the load parameter.
With \texttt{NLprebuck=True} the pre-buckling state is obtained from a
geometrically nonlinear analysis, and the whole expansion must then be carried
out about a state \emph{in the neighbourhood of the bifurcation point}.  This
document describes the latter case.

Throughout, $\lambda$ is the load parameter multiplying the reference load
$N_{xx}^{\text{unit}}$, $\bm{f}_0$ is the corresponding reference load vector,
$\lambda_b$ is the load level at which the pre-buckling state is evaluated,
and $\lambda_c$ is the bifurcation load.

%==============================================================================
\section{Kinematics and the discrete energy functional}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Strain measures}

The membrane strains are written, for both kinematic models, as
\begin{equation}
  \bm{\varepsilon}
  = \bm{B}_m\ub
  + \tfrac12
    \begin{Bmatrix} g_1^2 \\ g_2^2 \\ 2\,g_1 g_2 \end{Bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \bm{\kappa} = \bm{B}_b\ub ,
  \label{eq:strain}
\end{equation}
where $g_1$ and $g_2$ are the two gradient measures that carry the geometric
nonlinearity,
\begin{equation}
  \text{von K\'arm\'an:}\quad g_1 = w_{,x},\quad g_2 = w_{,y},
  \qquad
  \text{Sanders:}\quad g_1 = w_{,x},\quad g_2 = w_{,y} - \frac{v}{R} .
  \label{eq:g12}
\end{equation}
Every nonlinear term of the Sanders membrane strains is recovered from the von
K\'arm\'an one by the substitution $(w_{,x},w_{,y})\rightarrow(g_1,g_2)$, which
is why the two modules differ only in the definition~\eqref{eq:g12} and in the
corresponding interpolation matrices $\bm{G}_1,\bm{G}_2$ with
$g_\alpha = \bm{G}_\alpha\ub$.

The constitutive relation is the usual laminated-shell one,
$\bm{N} = \bm{A}\bm{\varepsilon} + \bm{B}\bm{\kappa}$ and
$\bm{M} = \bm{B}\bm{\varepsilon} + \bm{D}\bm{\kappa}$, collectively denoted
$H$.

\subsection{State and rates are independent fields}
\label{sec:staterates}

The pre-buckling state is a curve $\ub_0(\lambda)$.  The asymptotic expansion
needs, besides the state itself, its first and second derivatives with respect
to $\lambda$, written $\dd{\ub}_0$ and $\ddd{\ub}_0$.  Differentiating
\eqref{eq:strain} along the path, and abbreviating
$g_\alpha^s = \bm{G}_\alpha\ub_0$, $g_\alpha^d = \bm{G}_\alpha\dd{\ub}_0$,
$g_\alpha^{dd} = \bm{G}_\alpha\ddd{\ub}_0$,
\begin{align}
  \dd{\bm{\varepsilon}}
  &= \bm{B}_m\dd{\ub}_0
   + \begin{Bmatrix}
       g_1^s g_1^d \\ g_2^s g_2^d \\ g_1^s g_2^d + g_2^s g_1^d
     \end{Bmatrix},
  & \dd{\bm{\kappa}} &= \bm{B}_b\dd{\ub}_0,
  \label{eq:epsdot}\\[4pt]
  \ddd{\bm{\varepsilon}}
  &= \bm{B}_m\ddd{\ub}_0
   + \begin{Bmatrix}
       (g_1^d)^2 + g_1^s g_1^{dd} \\
       (g_2^d)^2 + g_2^s g_2^{dd} \\
       2 g_1^d g_2^d + g_1^s g_2^{dd} + g_2^s g_1^{dd}
     \end{Bmatrix},
  & \ddd{\bm{\kappa}} &= \bm{B}_b\ddd{\ub}_0,
  \label{eq:epsddot}
\end{align}
and the derivatives with respect to the nodal degrees of freedom
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\bm{\varepsilon}}{\partial\ub_a}
  = \bm{B}_m + \begin{Bmatrix}
      g_1^s\bm{G}_1 \\ g_2^s\bm{G}_2 \\
      g_1^s\bm{G}_2 + g_2^s\bm{G}_1 \end{Bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial^2\bm{\varepsilon}}{\partial\lambda\,\partial\ub_a}
  = \begin{Bmatrix}
      g_1^d\bm{G}_1 \\ g_2^d\bm{G}_2 \\
      g_1^d\bm{G}_2 + g_2^d\bm{G}_1 \end{Bmatrix}.
  \label{eq:epsderivs}
\end{equation}

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Remark.} If one assumes the pre-buckling path to be linear in the load
parameter, $\ub_0(\lambda) = \lambda\,\ub_0(1)$, then
$\dd{\ub}_0 = \ub_0(1)$, $\ddd{\ub}_0 = \bm{0}$ and
$g_\alpha^s = \lambda\,g_\alpha^d$, so \eqref{eq:epsdot}--\eqref{eq:epsderivs}
collapse onto expressions containing $\lambda$ explicitly.  That assumption is
precisely what removes the nonlinear pre-buckling behaviour from the
expansion, and it is not made here: state, first rate and second rate are
carried as three independent nodal fields.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Discrete energy derivatives}

Let $\phi$ be the total potential energy.  The following contractions are
assembled element by element and are the building blocks of the expansion:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
symbol in the code & meaning \\
\midrule
\texttt{phi2}                & $\phi_2 = \partial^2\phi/\partial\ub^2$, the tangent operator at the critical state \\
\texttt{phi3\_ab[(i,j)]}     & $\phi_3$ contracted with modes $i,j$, one free index \\
\texttt{phi4[(i,j,k,l)]}     & $\phi_4$ fully contracted \\
\texttt{phi20\_a[i]}         & $\partial\phi_2/\partial\lambda$ contracted with mode $i$, one free index \\
\texttt{phi30\_ab[(i,j)]}    & $\partial\phi_3/\partial\lambda$ contracted with modes $i,j$, one free index \\
\texttt{phi200\_ab[(i,j)]}   & $\partial^2\phi_2/\partial\lambda^2$ fully contracted \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
All of them are built from \eqref{eq:epsdot}--\eqref{eq:epsderivs} together
with the constant quadratic form
$\partial^2\bm{\varepsilon}/\partial\ub_a\partial\ub_b$, which for
\eqref{eq:strain} is
$\bm{G}_1^{\!\top}\bm{G}_1$, $\bm{G}_2^{\!\top}\bm{G}_2$ and
$\bm{G}_1^{\!\top}\bm{G}_2 + \bm{G}_2^{\!\top}\bm{G}_1$ in the three strain
components.

%==============================================================================
\section{Locating the expansion point}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Why the load level matters}

Koiter's expansion is asymptotic about the bifurcation point.  Sun~\S2.2
requires the pre-buckling state to satisfy
$\lambda_b/\lambda_c \approx 0.995$.  If instead the pre-buckling problem is
solved once at the reference load and the eigenvalue problem is posed about
that state, then for the shells considered here $\lambda_b/\lambda_c$ is of
the order of $0.1$.  At that load level the radial pre-buckling deflection is
one to three per cent of a wall thickness, the boundary layer produced by the
edge restraint has not developed, and
$\lVert\KCNL(\ub_0)\rVert/\lVert\KCz\rVert \sim 2\times10^{-4}$; the
eigenvalue analysis is then a linear buckling analysis in all but name and the
whole computation returns the \emph{membrane} pre-buckling answer.

\subsection{Iterative eigenvalue algorithm}

The expansion point is located by the incremental--iterative scheme of
Sun~Eqs.~(44)--(46).  At the current load level $\lambda_b$ the pre-buckling
state $\ub_0$ is known and the eigenvalue problem
\begin{equation}
  \big[\KC(\ub_0) + \mu\,\KG(\ub_0)\big]\,\ub_1 = \bm{0},
  \qquad
  \KC(\ub_0) = \KCz + \KCNL(\ub_0),
  \label{eq:eig}
\end{equation}
gives the multiplier $\mu$ \emph{of the current stress state}, so that
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_c = \lambda_b\,\mu .
  \label{eq:lambdac}
\end{equation}
Convergence is declared when
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\lambda_c-\lambda_b}{\lambda_c} \le \varepsilon_1 ,
  \label{eq:conv}
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon_1 = 0.005$ by default, and otherwise the load level is
advanced by
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_b \leftarrow \lambda_b + \eta\,(\lambda_c - \lambda_b),
  \qquad
  \eta = 0.8 \text{ on the first step},\quad \eta = 0.5 \text{ afterwards}.
  \label{eq:step}
\end{equation}
The larger first factor moves quickly into the neighbourhood of the buckling
load; the smaller subsequent one avoids overshooting it.

At convergence $\mu \simeq 1$ to within $\varepsilon_1$, which is what makes
the operators of \eqref{eq:eig} usable directly in the expansion
(Section~\ref{sec:consistency}).

\subsection{Pre-buckling solve near a singular tangent}
\label{sec:nr}

Each load step solves $\bm{f}_{\text{int}}(\ub) = \lambda_b\bm{f}_0$ by a
Newton--Raphson iteration on the tangent stiffness
$\Kt = \KCz + \KCNL(\ub) + \KG(\ub)$.  Close to the bifurcation point $\Kt$ is
nearly singular along the buckling modes, and a load-controlled iteration
diverges: any round-off component of the residual in those directions is
amplified and throws the iterate off the fundamental path.  Four measures are
used.

\paragraph{Tangent predictor.}  The starting point of a step is
$\ub_0 + \dd{\ub}_0\,\Delta\lambda$ rather than a rescaling of the previous
state.

\paragraph{Deflation of the buckling modes.}  The fundamental path of a
perfect cylinder under axial compression has no component along the buckling
modes, which are not axisymmetric.  The correction is therefore obtained from
the bordered system
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix} \Kt & \bm{Q} \\ \bm{Q}^{\!\top} & \bm{0}\end{bmatrix}
  \begin{Bmatrix} \delta\ub \\ \bm{\alpha}\end{Bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{Bmatrix} -\bm{R} \\ \bm{0}\end{Bmatrix},
  \label{eq:nrdeflate}
\end{equation}
with $\bm{Q}$ the currently estimated near-critical eigenvectors.  This keeps
$\delta\ub$ on the fundamental path.

\paragraph{Step back-off.}  If the iteration diverges, $\eta$ in
\eqref{eq:step} is halved and the step retried.  Divergence is detected early,
as soon as the residual measure exceeds $0.5$, so that a failed trial costs a
few iterations rather than the full iteration budget.

\paragraph{Best iterate on stall.}  Very close to the bifurcation point the
residual of the deflated iteration reaches a floor above the convergence
tolerance and then grows again.  The best iterate is retained whenever that
floor is below a looser acceptance tolerance.

Even with these measures the load-controlled scheme does not always reach
$\lambda_b/\lambda_c = 0.995$; the value actually achieved is reported, and
the analysis proceeds from the best state obtained.  Reaching the target
reliably requires arc-length control on the fundamental path, or the
extrapolated eigenvalue problem of Sun~Eq.~(27), neither of which is
implemented (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\subsection{Rates of the pre-buckling state}

The first rate follows from Sun~Eq.~(29), using the tangent stiffness at the
converged pre-buckling state,
\begin{equation}
  \Kt\,\dd{\ub}_0 = \bm{f}_0 ,
  \label{eq:qdot}
\end{equation}
solved with the same deflation \eqref{eq:nrdeflate} for the same reason.  The
second rate follows from Sun~Eq.~(35) as a backward difference against the
load step preceding the converged one,
\begin{equation}
  \ddd{\ub}_0 = \frac{\dd{\ub}_0 - \dd{\ub}_0^{\,\text{prev}}}
                     {\lambda_b - \lambda_b^{\,\text{prev}}} .
  \label{eq:qddot}
\end{equation}
Reusing a step the load stepping has already converged on costs nothing, and,
unlike a step solved ahead of $\lambda_b$, cannot fall on the far side of the
bifurcation point.  The corresponding stress rates
$\dd{\bm{\sigma}}$ and $\ddd{\bm{\sigma}}$, Sun~Eqs.~(30) and~(34), follow from
\eqref{eq:epsdot} and \eqref{eq:epsddot} through $H$.

%==============================================================================
\section{Consistency of the operators}
\label{sec:consistency}
%==============================================================================

The operator appearing in the asymptotic expansion,
\begin{equation}
  \phi_2 = \KC(\ub_0) + \mu\,\KG(\ub_0),
  \label{eq:phi2}
\end{equation}
must be \emph{the same} operator whose null vector is the buckling mode, that
is, exactly the operator of the eigenvalue problem~\eqref{eq:eig}.  Building
$\phi_2$ from a differently evaluated $\KCNL$ than the one used in
\eqref{eq:eig} --- for instance from $\KCNL(\lambda_c\ub_0)$ while the
eigenvalue problem used $\KCNL(\ub_0)$ --- leaves $\phi_2$ non-singular along
$\ub_1$ and corrupts every quantity derived from it.  The severity is easy to
measure: with the inconsistent pairing,
\[
  \frac{\lVert(\bm{K}_{C,\text{eig}} + \mu\KG)\ub_1\rVert}
       {\lVert\KCz\ub_1\rVert}
  \approx 10^{-10},
  \qquad
  \frac{\lVert\phi_2\ub_1\rVert}{\lVert\KCz\ub_1\rVert} \approx 4.6 ,
\]
i.e.\ the expansion operator does not annihilate the buckling mode at all.
Since $\lambda_b\simeq\lambda_c$ after the iterative algorithm has converged,
$\mu\simeq1$ and \eqref{eq:phi2} is simply the tangent operator at the
critical state.

\section{Second-order fields and the orthogonality condition}
\label{sec:ortho}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{The singular system}

The second-order fields $\ub_{ij}$ solve
\begin{equation}
  \phi_2\,\ub_{ij} = \bm{g}_{ij},
  \qquad
  \bm{g}_{ij} = -\tfrac12\,\phi_{3,ij}
                - \frac{1}{m}\sum_{k} a_{kij}\,\lambda_k\,\phi_{20,k},
  \label{eq:secondorder}
\end{equation}
with $m$ the number of Koiter modes.  By construction $\phi_2$ is singular:
its null space is spanned by the buckling modes.  Consequently
\eqref{eq:secondorder} determines $\ub_{ij}$ only up to an arbitrary element
of that null space, and a plain sparse solve followed by a projection is not a
well-posed procedure.  Two things are therefore needed: a solver that copes
with the singularity, and the correct condition that fixes the null-space
component.

\subsection{The condition to be imposed}

The orthogonality condition of Koiter's theory is Sun~Eq.~(18),
\begin{equation}
  \dd{\bm{\sigma}}_c\!\cdot\! L_{11}(\uv_1,\uv_2)
  + \bm{\sigma}_1\!\cdot\! L_{11}(\dd{\uv}_c,\uv_2)
  + \bm{\sigma}_2\!\cdot\! L_{11}(\dd{\uv}_c,\uv_1) = 0 ,
  \label{eq:sun18}
\end{equation}
whose finite element form is Sun~Eq.~(33),
\begin{equation}
  \ub_k^{\!\top}
  \big[\bm{K}_D(\ub_b,\dd{\ub}_b) + \KG(\dd{\bm\sigma}_b)\big]\ub_{ij}
  + \tfrac12\,
  \ub_k^{\!\top}\bm{B}_{NL}^{\!\top}(\ub_k)\,H\,\bm{B}_{NL}(\ub_k)\,\dd{\ub}_b
  = 0 .
  \label{eq:sun33}
\end{equation}
Two observations turn this into something directly computable.

\paragraph{The linear term is already assembled.}  The bracket in the first
term is the load-parameter derivative of the tangent stiffness,
\[
  \bm{K}_D(\ub_b,\dd{\ub}_b) + \KG(\dd{\bm\sigma}_b)
  = \frac{\partial \phi_2}{\partial\lambda} ,
\]
so the first term is $\ub_k^{\!\top}(\partial\phi_2/\partial\lambda)\ub_{ij}$,
which is exactly the contraction stored as \texttt{phi20\_a[k]}:
\begin{equation}
  \text{first term} = \phi_{20,k}\cdot\ub_{ij}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The constant term follows from $\phi_{30}$.}  With
$\bm{B}_{NL}(\ub)\bm{v} = L_{11}(\ub,\bm{v})$ the second term of
\eqref{eq:sun33} is
\begin{equation}
  \tfrac12\,\big\langle L_2(\uv_k),\,H\,L_{11}(\uv_k,\dd{\uv}_0)\big\rangle
  \;=\; \tfrac12\,I ,
  \qquad
  I = \int \big\langle \bm{N}[L_2(\uv_k)],\,
                       L_{11}(\dd{\uv}_0,\uv_k)\big\rangle .
\end{equation}
Now $\phi_{30,ij}$ is assembled as one half of the quadrature weight times the
sum of six contractions of the form
$\langle\bm{N}[\,\cdot\,],\,\partial^2\bm{\varepsilon}/
\partial\lambda\partial\ub\,\rangle$.  When all mode indices coincide those
six contributions are equal to one another, by the symmetry of $H$ and of the
quadratic form, so that
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{30,ij}\cdot\ub_k = \tfrac12\cdot 6\,I = 3\,I
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  \boxed{\;c_k \;=\; \tfrac12 I \;=\;
         \frac{\phi_{30,ij}\cdot\ub_k}{6}\;}
  \label{eq:cconst}
\end{equation}
No new element integral is required.  The condition to impose is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{20,k}\cdot\ub_{ij} = -\,c_k .
  \label{eq:condition}
\end{equation}
It is weighted by the stiffness matrices, through $\phi_{20}$, and
inhomogeneous, through $c_k$.  A Gram--Schmidt projection, or equivalently a
symmetric Euclidean border $\bm{Q}^{\!\top}\ub_{ij} = \bm{0}$, imposes neither
of these properties and is the procedure that \eqref{eq:condition} replaces.

\subsection{The bordered system}

Both requirements are met by a \emph{non-symmetric} bordered system,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \phi_2 & \bm{N}_{\!sp} \\[2pt]
    \bm{W}^{\!\top} & \bm{0}
  \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{Bmatrix} \ub_{ij} \\ \bm{\alpha} \end{Bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{Bmatrix} \bm{g}_{ij} \\ -\bm{c} \end{Bmatrix},
  \label{eq:bordered}
\end{equation}
in which the roles of the two borders are distinct:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item the \emph{column} border $\bm{N}_{\!sp}$ spans the null space of
        $\phi_2$ and is what makes the system non-singular;
  \item the \emph{row} border $\bm{W}$ carries the constraints, one column per
        Koiter mode, $\bm{W}[:,k] = \phi_{20,k}$, with the corresponding
        right-hand side $-c_k$ from \eqref{eq:cconst}.
\end{itemize}
The system is non-singular provided $\bm{W}^{\!\top}\bm{N}_{\!sp}$ is, and its
leading entry $\phi_{20,k}\cdot\ub_k$ is exactly the quantity that appears in
the denominator of the post-buckling coefficients, hence non-zero by
construction.

\subsection{Degenerate modes}
\label{sec:degenerate}

For a circular cylinder the buckling modes occur in degenerate pairs, one for
each sign of the circumferential wave number, so $\dim\ker\phi_2 \ge 2$ even
when a single Koiter mode is retained.  Two consequences follow.

First, $\bm{N}_{\!sp}$ must span the \emph{whole} near-null space, not only the
retained Koiter modes: every eigenvector whose multiplier equals the critical
one within a relative tolerance is included, and the set is orthonormalised
with a rank-revealing factorisation.  Bordering with the Koiter modes alone
leaves \eqref{eq:bordered} singular along the remaining directions.

Second, \eqref{eq:sun33} supplies one condition per Koiter mode, which is
fewer than $\dim\ker\phi_2$.  The degenerate partners lie outside single-mode
theory, and the remaining rows of $\bm{W}$ keep the Euclidean condition, built
from the null-space directions orthogonal to the retained modes.  This is a
choice, not a result; the consistent treatment of a degenerate critical mode
is the multi-mode Koiter expansion.

\subsection{What the weighted condition does, and does not, change}

Condition \eqref{eq:condition} is the correct one and replaces a Gram--Schmidt
projection that was neither weighted nor inhomogeneous.  For the cases
considered here, however, it changes almost nothing.  Running the same
analysis under the two conditions, with everything else identical, gives
\[
  \frac{\lVert\ub_{ij}^{\text{Eq.}(33)}-\ub_{ij}^{\text{eucl}}\rVert}
       {\lVert\ub_{ij}\rVert} = 6.5\times10^{-7},
  \qquad
  b_{1111}: -1.57604660 \;\rightarrow\; -1.57604530 .
\]
The second-order field itself barely moves, so the two conditions are
numerically near-coincident for this problem: the null-space component of
$\ub_{ij}$ on which they disagree is small to begin with.

A second reason reinforces this, and would survive a larger difference.
Condition \eqref{eq:condition} fixes only the component of $\ub_{ij}$ along
$\ker\phi_2$, and $b_{ijkl}$ sees $\ub_{ij}$ through the contractions
$\phi_{3,ij}\cdot\ub_{kl}$; but $\phi_{3,ij}$ contracted with a buckling mode
is precisely the numerator of $a_{ijk}$ in \eqref{eq:a}.  For the symmetric
bifurcation of a cylinder under axial compression $a_{ijk}$ vanishes,
measured values being of order $10^{-5}$ against a $b_{ijkl}$ of order unity,
so $\phi_{3,ij}$ is nearly orthogonal to the null space.

Either way the conclusion is the same: the weighted condition is necessary for
correctness, but it is not the resolution of the ambiguity discussed above,
where the spread between two members of the degenerate pair remains a factor
of $1.5$.  For an asymmetric bifurcation, with $a_{ijk}\neq0$, it would carry
more weight.

A related practical point: the basis that an iterative eigensolver returns for
a degenerate eigenspace is arbitrary.  ARPACK keeps its random seed in a saved
variable, so without an explicit starting vector the basis --- and with it
$\ub_{ij}$ and $b_{ijkl}$ --- depends on how many eigenvalue problems have
been solved before in the same process.  A fixed starting vector is therefore
passed to the eigensolver, which makes the results reproducible; it does not
remove the underlying ambiguity, which only multi-mode theory resolves.

%==============================================================================
\section{Post-buckling coefficients}
%==============================================================================

With the second-order fields available, the coefficients are
\begin{equation}
  a_{ijk} = -\frac{1}{2\lambda_i}\,
            \frac{\phi_{3,ij}\cdot\ub_k}{\phi_{20,i}\cdot\ub_i},
  \label{eq:a}
\end{equation}
\begin{multline}
  b_{ijkl} = -\frac{1}{6\,\lambda_i\,(\phi_{20,i}\cdot\ub_i)}
  \Big[\,
    \phi_{4,ijkl}
    + 3\,\phi_{3,ij}\cdot\ub_{kl}
    + 3\,\phi_{3,il}\cdot\ub_{jk} \\
    + \lambda_i\big(
        a_{iij}\,\phi_{30,ik}\!\cdot\!\ub_l
      + a_{ijk}\,\phi_{30,il}\!\cdot\!\ub_i
      + a_{ikl}\,\phi_{30,ii}\!\cdot\!\ub_j \big) \\
    + \phi_{200,ii}\,\lambda_i^2\big(
        a_{iij}a_{ikl} + a_{ijk}a_{ili} + a_{ikl}a_{iij} \big)
  \Big].
  \label{eq:b}
\end{multline}
The buckling modes are normalised so that the largest translational amplitude
equals the shell thickness, which is the normalisation used by the reference
solutions.

%==============================================================================
\section{Algorithm}
%==============================================================================

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Koiter analysis about a nonlinear pre-buckling state}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State solve $\KCz\ub = \bm{f}_0$ for the linear pre-buckling state;
       $\lambda_b \gets 1$
\For{$i = 1,\dots,i_{\max}$}
  \State assemble $\KC(\ub_0)$, $\KG(\ub_0)$
  \State solve the eigenvalue problem \eqref{eq:eig} $\rightarrow \mu$,
         $\ub_1$; \quad $\lambda_c \gets \lambda_b\mu$
  \State \textbf{if} \eqref{eq:conv} holds \textbf{then break}
  \State $\eta \gets 0.8$ if $i=1$ else $0.5$
  \Repeat
    \State $\lambda_b^{\text{new}} \gets \lambda_b + \eta(\lambda_c-\lambda_b)$
    \State predictor $\ub_0 + \dd{\ub}_0(\lambda_b^{\text{new}}-\lambda_b)$;
           Newton--Raphson with deflation \eqref{eq:nrdeflate}
    \State \textbf{on failure} $\eta \gets \eta/2$
  \Until{converged or $\eta$ exhausted}
  \State store $(\lambda_b,\dd{\ub}_0)$ as the previous step; advance
\EndFor
\State $\dd{\ub}_0$ from \eqref{eq:qdot}; $\ddd{\ub}_0$ from \eqref{eq:qddot}
\State assemble $\phi_2,\phi_3,\phi_4,\phi_{20},\phi_{30},\phi_{200}$ using
       \eqref{eq:epsdot}--\eqref{eq:epsderivs}
\State $a_{ijk}$ from \eqref{eq:a}
\State $\ub_{ij}$ from the bordered system \eqref{eq:bordered} with the
       weighted condition \eqref{eq:condition}
\State $b_{ijkl}$ from \eqref{eq:b}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Effect on the results}
%==============================================================================

The two verification cases are the composite cylinder of Sun~\S3.1 and the
NASA shell AW-CYL-1-1 of Arbocz, Starnes and Nemeth (AIAA-2001-1392); they are
the same shell, so the ANILISA $n$-search of the latter provides reference
values for both.  Buckling loads are normalised by
$N_{c\ell} = E_{11}t^2/\big(R\sqrt{3(1-\nu_{12}^2)}\big)$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
 & expanding at $N_{xx}^{\text{unit}}$ & about $\lambda_b\simeq\lambda_c$
 & ANILISA, nonlinear pre-buckling \\
\midrule
$\lambda_b/\lambda_c$      & $0.14$  & $0.985$ & --- \\
$N_{cr}/N_{c\ell}$         & $0.3581$& $0.3367$& $0.337088$ ($n=7$) \\
max $|w_0|/t$              & $0.026$ & $0.192$ & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The first column is the membrane pre-buckling result, which is what the
computation returns when the expansion point is left at the reference load;
the reference value for that regime is $0.365992$.  The second column agrees
with the ANILISA nonlinear pre-buckling entry for the same circumferential
wave number to $0.1\%$.

The post-buckling coefficient is far more sensitive to mesh refinement than
the buckling load, and the reference value $b=-0.376$ belongs to the $n=11$
edge-buckling mode, so a like-for-like comparison requires a mesh fine enough
for that mode to become critical.  On the finest mesh run here the magnitude
$|b| = 0.437$ is of the right order, against $-0.37605$ from ANILISA and
$-0.3772$ from Sun~Table~2, where expanding at the reference load had given
$-0.0598$, close to the membrane pre-buckling value $-0.048844$.

Its \emph{sign}, however, is not determined.  Two runs of the same mesh that
differ only in the member of the degenerate pair returned by the eigensolver
gave $-0.43100$ and $+0.43731$, with $\lambda_c$, the wave number and
$\lambda_b/\lambda_c$ identical.  Since $b<0$ means an imperfection-sensitive
shell and $b>0$ the opposite, this is a substantive limitation of a
single-mode expansion about a degenerate critical mode, not solver noise; see
Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

%==============================================================================
\section{Known limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
%==============================================================================

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
  \item \textbf{Load control near the bifurcation point.}  The scheme of
        Section~\ref{sec:nr} reaches $\lambda_b/\lambda_c$ between about
        $0.93$ and $0.985$ rather than the $0.995$ that Sun~\S2.2 asks for.
        Arc-length control on the fundamental path, or the extrapolated
        eigenvalue problem of Sun~Eq.~(27),
        $[\Kt + (\lambda_c-\lambda_b)\,\partial\Kt/\partial\lambda]\ub_1=0$,
        would remove the need to sit on top of the bifurcation point.
  \item \textbf{Degenerate critical modes.}  As discussed in
        Section~\ref{sec:degenerate}, the condition \eqref{eq:condition}
        supplies one equation per retained mode while the null space is
        larger.  The observable consequence is that the sign of $b_{ijkl}$
        depends on which member of the degenerate pair is retained: the same
        mesh yields $-0.43100$ or $+0.43731$ according to the basis the
        eigensolver returns.  Fixing the eigensolver starting vector makes a
        given run reproducible but does not resolve the ambiguity.  Neither
        does \eqref{eq:condition}: see the remark at the end of
        Section~\ref{sec:ortho}.  Multi-mode Koiter is the consistent
        treatment.
  \item \textbf{Symmetrisation of $\phi_{30}$ for distinct modes.}  The
        identity \eqref{eq:cconst} was established for coinciding mode
        indices, which covers the single-mode case exercised here; the
        general multi-mode symmetrisation remains to be worked out.
  \item \textbf{Quadrature.}  Convergence with respect to the number of Gauss
        points has been verified: $n_{int}=4$ gives $b$ to within $0.4\%$ of
        $n_{int}=5$ and $6$, while $n_{int}=2$ under-integrates and produces a
        rank-deficient element.
\end{enumerate}
